What this mockup is testing

Home is the wall, and the wall is the navigation
Tap a row on the board to reach the controller that drives it. The physical object is the menu — there is no settings tab to find your way back out of.
Settings live next to the thing they affect
Flap speed and line-up belong to the row, so that is where they sit. Name, time zone and role belong to the box. Reflashing belongs to the units. That is the part that makes this a different stance rather than a different set of doors — a settings section would have collected all of them in one place, away from what they change.
One flap size for the whole wall
Every row is drawn at the widest row's flap size, so five units next to sixteen look like five units next to sixteen. Nothing stretches to fill.
Two object types, kept apart
.20 drives no row, so it has nothing on the board. It says so in its own words on its page rather than appearing as an empty row.
State changes attention, never what exists
Flip the four states: the same wall, the same three controllers, every time. A silent row still shows the text it is holding. The Controllers line is always there — it just stops being quiet when something needs you.
A number shown without crying wolf
Row 0's unit 5 has turned 1257 times against 13 for its neighbours. WearPolicy.h correctly does not flag it — flagging starts at max(2 × median, median + 10000), so 10,013 here. The console shows the number and says plainly that it is not a fault, instead of either hiding it or dressing it as an alarm.
Who last drove the wall
The line under the board names the source. Firmware has no such field yet — DisplayCommand carries no origin across its eight enqueue sites. This is the argument for adding one.
